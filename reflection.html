Reflection Page – Personal Budget Project

By Zoie Schneider

1. Budget Reflection

After completing my budget, I had a small surplus remaining each month. I believe the best thing to do with this extra money is to put it into savings. Even a little bit each month can add up over time and help prepare for emergencies or future expenses, like vacations, gifts, or even a down payment on a home. Saving also gives me peace of mind and helps build financial independence.

2. Current Job and Spending

I currently do not hold a job, but I have had part-time work experiences helping family or volunteering. When I do earn money, I typically spend it on personal items like hair, nails, snacks, and sometimes gas or small gifts. I know that if I had a job earning minimum wage, it would not be enough to live on comfortably. That’s why I believe graduating from high school and going to college is so important. Higher education can open more doors and lead to better-paying career options with stability and growth.

3. Lessons Learned

Three parts of this project that stood out to me were:

  • Learning how much money is really needed to live independently
  • How taxes and deductions lower your actual paycheck
  • How much difference smart spending and planning can make

It surprised me how fast things add up—like car payments, rent, and groceries. I now understand why adults budget carefully and why it’s important to keep track of every dollar.

4. Fears About Real Life

The part of real life that intimidates me the most is managing bills and staying out of debt. It seems overwhelming to keep up with rent, credit, utilities, and savings all at once. I plan to overcome this challenge by learning more about financial planning, staying organized, and asking for help when needed. Right now, I can prepare by practicing smart money habits—like saving my allowance, comparing prices, and not buying things I don’t need.

5. Financial Concerns and Curiosity

I still have concerns about credit scores, student loans, and investing. I wish I knew more about how credit works and how to build it responsibly. I also want to learn more about how taxes are filed each year. I plan to learn about these topics by talking with my parents, watching YouTube videos, and using trusted websites like NerdWallet or Investopedia. Financial literacy is something I want to keep learning so I can be confident and independent in the future.
